## Related work since the announcement

The 9 September preprint [Distribution of Singular Data Generated by Compact
Forced Navier–Stokes Blowup](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10262) studies consequences
of inserting a compact forced singular packet. Its abstract specifically says
that it varies the forcing and does not classify singular initial data for a
single prescribed force. It is directly relevant to understanding how
exceptional forced singularities can sit inside broad function-space classes,
but it supplies no molecular model and no particle-position or alignment
conclusion.

The 20 September preprint [The Positive Defect Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23868)
sets a separate target for an **unforced** periodic Leray–Hopf blowup search and
states that unforced alternatives remain open. Its abstract also warns that a
finite computation alone cannot witness its Galerkin-uniform criterion. This
helps keep the forced construction distinct from ordinary unforced fluid
behavior; it is a preprint, not a settled consensus result.

## Consequences for the molecular alignment hypothesis

The new source estimates and the existing conditional axis calculation are
continuum statements about fields, derivatives, and a specially constructed
smooth force. Divergence or growth of a continuum field near a singular time
does not itself imply that molecules become spatially ordered or that a
particle-position distribution becomes more concentrated. Such an implication
requires an explicit microscopic/kinetic bridge: a defined particle ensemble,
scaling limit, observable for alignment, and a derivation linking the
continuum solution and forcing to that observable. None is present in the
reviewed Lean source or the cited preprints. No phase transition or change in
constitutive viscosity follows from the current results.
