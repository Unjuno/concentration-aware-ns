# Audit of OpenAI's Navier–Stokes and Euler result

## Source identity and reproducibility status

The relevant public repository is [`openai/NavierStokesAndEuler`](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler), pinned for this audit at commit `f9e8bc5b38b6e212696e8a30e3e91517af887bbd`. GitHub reports the repository as public, Apache-2.0 licensed, with two commits and no open issues at the time of inspection. Its README describes Lean 4 formalizations accompanying OpenAI's Navier–Stokes and Euler papers, and gives `lake exe cache get` followed by `lake build` as the reproduction path. It also points to Comparator challenges for an independent proof-checking route.

The official paper is [Finite Time Blowup for Navier–Stokes](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/32d9f210-8b73-45e0-91bc-82a30aef8a9a/navier-stokes.pdf); the announcement is [On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/). The paper's theorem states that for every positive viscosity there is a smooth compactly supported force and a solution starting from rest that is smooth for each time before the singular time, has uniformly bounded kinetic energy, and has unbounded `L∞` velocity as the singular time is approached. This is a theorem about a constructed forced continuum Navier–Stokes solution, not a numerical simulation of an observed material.

As of this audit, the [Clay Mathematics Institute's problem page](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/) still lists Navier–Stokes among its active problems. OpenAI says it does not intend to claim the Millennium Prize for this result. This distinguishes the announced proof claim and accompanying formalization from an official prize disposition; it says nothing by itself about whether the mathematics is correct.

The repository was cloned at the pinned commit and its declared Lean 4.34.0-rc2 toolchain was available locally. The local `lake exe cache get` and `lake build` attempts both terminated with macOS `SIGTRAP` / `EXC_BREAKPOINT` (exit 133) inside Lake while materializing dependencies; the crash report shows an Apple malloc abort during pthread cleanup. Dependencies were then fetched manually at the manifest's pinned revisions and a single further `lake exe cache get` attempt failed identically. This is an environment/toolchain execution failure, not a Lean theorem counterexample. No successful independent local rebuild is claimed. A separate Linux Actions check has now been added to build the pinned repository, run the independent Nanoda checker with `sorryAx` disallowed, and audit both theorem namespaces for unapproved axioms; its result is pending. These checks cannot replace expert mathematical review. A successful Lean kernel build establishes the formalized propositions under their imported definitions and axioms, not physical applicability beyond those propositions.

## What the constructed flow says

Let `τ = 1 − t` be time to blow-up. The paper describes a shrinking core with radial scale `ℓr ≍ τ^(1/2)` and axial scale `ℓz ≍ τ^(1/2−h)`, for `0 < h < 1/100`. Thus `ℓr/ℓz ≍ τ^h → 0`: the core becomes a slender column. Typical azimuthal and axial velocity scales grow like `τ^(−1/2−h)` while radial velocity is `O(τ^(−1/2))`; the core's kinetic energy tends to zero. The described motion spirals inward and has axial outflow on opposite sides of a dividing layer. It is not a claim that individual molecules become ordered or line up.

The paper also distinguishes Reynolds-number directions. Its angular Reynolds number grows like `τ^(−h)`, but the radial Reynolds number remains `O(1)`; radial transport, radial diffusion, and axial transport remain in the leading balance. That does not support the claim that positive viscosity suddenly loses its effect. The construction deliberately retains viscosity in the equations and in the radial balance.

The announcement notes that an actual material cannot attain infinite speed and that the continuum description would then break down. The theorem itself contains no molecular degrees of freedom, equation of state, heat transport, cavitation, phase transition, or particle-position predictions. Mapping its shrinking continuum scales to a real liquid or gas requires physical parameters and a coupled microscopic or compressible model; the paper supplies no universal molecular cutoff or transition prediction.

The canonical repository already contains a conditional material-kinematics calculation for a selected axis trajectory of the formal construction. There, infinitesimal material directions align under the volume-preserving linearized deformation, but an initially isotropic Gaussian packet has covariance eigenvalues `σ²Q^C, σ²Q^C, σ²Q^(-2C)`: its determinant and differential entropy stay constant, while the probability inside any fixed-radius ball centered on the packet tends to zero. This exact statement belongs to the linearized Gaussian model, not a finite-size particle experiment. It directly separates directional alignment from position certainty; see `docs/openai-core-material-trajectory.md` and `docs/affine-alignment-viscosity-counterexample.md`.

## Developments after the announcement

Two September arXiv follow-ups add mathematical context, but both explicitly start from the OpenAI construction. Cao, Chi, and Nie's revised preprint [Density of Forces Producing Navier–Stokes Blowup](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10262) (v4, September 22) claims that for fixed viscosity and zero initial velocity, smooth blow-up-producing forces are dense in a relative `L¹_t Hˢ_x` topology exactly when `s < 1/2`, on both the torus and whole space. The authors describe the OpenAI compactly forced blow-up solution as their starting point, so this is an extension conditional on that construction, not independent confirmation or evidence that a laboratory forcing reaches the blow-up regime. Lei and Ren's [Part I profile-construction preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35406) (September 28) gives a more accessible development of the leading self-similar profile; its abstract says oscillatory-pulse residual correction is to be handled in a companion Part II. Neither preprint models molecular organization or phase change.

A public [AMReX/PhiFlow finite-surrogate project](https://github.com/cmccomb/navier-stokes-singularity-simulation) now publishes a high-resolution run toward normalized `t*=1`. Its README labels the computation a finite surrogate rather than the paper's exact infinite pulse hierarchy. It reports a 32³→64³→128³ endpoint velocity-field gap of 4.54% and forcing-field gap of 18.19%; at sampled active times, the finest-pair velocity gap is 2.19–2.47%, while forcing exceeds its stated 5% screen after about `t=0.629`. It further reports that the sampled global peaks are outside the finest core and the 128³ sampled vorticity maximum is about 9–10% above the 64³ value. The README explicitly says nearly identical peak speeds do not establish field convergence, that discrete forcing and diagnostic quadrature depend on the mesh, and that archive validation does not certify singularity formation. These are repository-reported results, not independently replayed here. The project is a useful numerical companion and reinforces why our own solver runs need exact-force readback, spatial/time refinement, local-QoI checks, and archive replay; it does not verify the theorem or establish a physical singularity.

A second, independent [pseudo-spectral surrogate](https://github.com/CokieMiner/nsblowup) was added at source commit `658812f7b02ea00065c54818e699105914507f9c` (Apache-2.0). Its README explicitly describes an MMS-style, finite-scale trajectory: a prescribed reference field generates the discrete residual forcing, runs stop at a positive clock floor, and the computation is not the paper's witness or a blow-up proof. It reports that its non-axisymmetric correction-wave optimizer returns zero amplitudes at `N≤128` because the wave carrier reaches the retained spectral cutoff; thus those runs do not numerically resolve the correction mechanism. The `artifacts-v1` release tag resolved to the same commit. Three small published assets were downloaded and their hashes matched GitHub's release-asset SHA-256 values: `wave_calibration.json` records four zero coefficients and identical residual energy before/after (zero reduction), while the accompanying `reference_report.txt` labels its carriers unresolved and states that the Newton estimate plus coarse log scan is not a global optimum certificate. The published `verify_report.txt` covers its stated 32³ MMS checks. These archived publisher artifacts are not an independent run or reproduction. The README's reported unforced release disperses, consistent with a driven surrogate; none of these results model molecular organization.

One README sentence generalizes the growth of the angular Reynolds number (`Re_theta ~ q^{-h}`) to “viscosity becomes asymptotically subdominant in the core” and “effectively Euler-dominated.” The OpenAI paper's Section 7.2 says viscosity remains leading-order in the pulse equation and gives `ε k² ≍ 1` through the correction process. The directional scaling alone does not imply core-wide disappearance of viscous effects. We reported this narrowly as a documentation-scope suggestion in [issue #1](https://github.com/CokieMiner/nsblowup/issues/1), not as a simulation defect or evidence of material viscosity collapse. The issue was checked against the repository's then-empty issue tracker and cites both pinned README lines and the primary paper. The three hash-verified public diagnostic assets are preserved under `evidence/external-nsblowup-658812f/`.

In this limited search of primary and project sources, I found these proof-dependent mathematical extensions and finite numerical demonstrations, but no independent formal audit of the OpenAI proof or a validated molecular/thermodynamic consequence. That is a bounded search result, not proof that no such work exists.

## How this relates to our numerical audit

This result strengthens the rationale for testing whether common numerical acceptance criteria can miss local concentration. It does not validate our OpenFOAM, SU2, or PhysicsNeMo test cases automatically. Those are separate manufactured-solution or model audits with their own forcing, discretization, implementation, convergence, and archive-integrity checks. The Navier–Stokes proof uses a specially constructed smooth force with increasingly fine, oscillatory internal momentum-flux corrections; a finite CFD result near a sharp feature cannot certify the singularity, and numerical divergence cannot distinguish the theorem's mechanism from ordinary under-resolution or a faulty solver setup.

Our SU2 run `37190363204` remains incomplete (one of five cases), and the first completed coarse case fails both its ordinary stopping test and local sampled-quality test. It is not a reproduced acceptance blind spot. The revised same-image parallel matrix is running as Actions run `37230949147`; its completion and independent archive replay are still required before changing this assessment. OpenFOAM, SU2, and PhysicsNeMo reports remain conditional on their exact executed gates.

## Disposition

No defect report or issue against OpenAI's repository is warranted by this audit. The source repository is a Lean proof formalization, not a CFD solver, and the current numerical evidence does not identify an error in the proof or a verified physical consequence. The useful next work is to finish the frozen CFD/ML matrices, perform the independent proof audit, and keep mathematical blow-up, a continuum-to-molecular transition, and engineering-system reachability as separate claims.
